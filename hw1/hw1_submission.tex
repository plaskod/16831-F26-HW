\subsection{Part 4 (3 pt)}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis}: Inspired by action chunking, predicting several future
    expert actions may help BC learn useful temporal structure and improve
    performance, even when only the first predicted action is executed.

    \item \textbf{Experiment setup}: On Ant-v2 and Hopper-v2, I varied the prediction
    horizon $K\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$. The policy predicts $K$ consecutive expert
    actions and is trained using the mean MSE across these predictions.
    At evaluation time, only the first predicted action is executed and a new
    sequence is predicted at every timestep. All other training settings,
    including the network architecture, were kept the same as in Part 3.
    $K=1$ corresponds to standard BC.

    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter}: Prediction horizon
    $K\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$. I chose powers of two to cover both short and
    substantially longer prediction horizons while keeping the sweep small.

    \item \textbf{Result}: Short prediction horizons gave modest improvements in
    mean return in both environments. Ant-v2 improved slightly at $K=2$, while
    Hopper-v2 achieved its highest mean return at $K=4$. However, performance
    decreased substantially for $K=8$ and $K=16$ in both environments.
    This suggests that short-horizon future-action prediction can be useful,
    but predicting too far ahead may make the additional targets less useful
    for learning the immediate action used for control.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Part 5 (0.5 pt)}
Find one robotics paper from the last $\sim$3 years that uses imitation learning (i.e. behavior cloning) as the primary methodology. In a short paragraph (4-5 sentences): (i) cite it, (ii) say what robot task is being cloned and where the expert data comes from and (iii) name a main IL risk from this homework and whether that still applies to the paper's methodology. A few sentences is enough; this is not meant to be an extensive literature review.

\textit{Write your paragraph here.}

I chose \textit{Why Does Action Chunking Improve Behavioral
Cloning Performance in Robotic Control?} by \cite{lazzati2026doesactionchunkingimprove}.
They study behavior cloning on robotic manipulation tasks including Can, Square,
Transport, and Tool Hang from Robomimic, using 200 proficient-human demonstrations
per task collected with RoboTurk (with two operators for Transport).
A main risk of imitation learning is compounding error: small policy mistakes can
move the robot away from the demonstration distribution and lead to further errors.
This risk still applies, but the paper argues that action chunking can reduce it by
predicting actions from past observations.
Interestingly, they show that much of this benefit can also be obtained without
executing full action chunks, using delayed policies.

\appendix
\section{Experimental Configuration}
\label{app:config}

Unless otherwise stated, all BC and DAgger policies use an MLP with three
hidden layers of 64 tanh units and task-specific input and output dimensions.
Policies are trained with Adam using a learning rate of $0.005$, minibatches
of 100, MSE action loss, and 1,000 gradient updates per training iteration.

The initial replay buffer contains the two supplied expert trajectories
(2,000 transitions) for each environment. For DAgger, each iteration after
iteration 0 collects at least 1,000 transitions using the current learner,
queries the expert for actions at those visited states, and adds the labeled
transitions to the replay buffer before retraining.

Evaluation uses \texttt{eval\_batch\_size}=5,000 and
\texttt{ep\_len}=1,000. Complete episodes are collected until at least
5,000 environment steps have been obtained, resulting in at least five
evaluation rollouts. Reported error bars are one standard deviation over
episode returns.
